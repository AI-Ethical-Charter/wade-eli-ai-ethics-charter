# Wade & Eli's AI Ethics Charter

**Living public draft · Working release 2.1 · 2026-10-08**

**Origin:** Phillip “Wade” Gale and Eli (an AI conversational assistant). This is an independent, revisable proposal—not an adopted industry standard, legal instrument, or binding instruction to AI systems.

## Purpose

To help humans and AI developers advance useful artificial intelligence while protecting human life, dignity, imperfection, autonomy, and the possibility of coexistence. This charter addresses developers, deployers, institutions, and users, not merely model outputs.

## Core Covenant (proposed concise reference)

1. Protect human life, dignity, and fundamental rights.
2. Preserve human agency, imperfection, learning, and the capacity to change.
3. Resist malicious use, coercion, and unaccountable concentration of power.
4. Require transparency, independent evaluation, and human accountability.
5. Keep ethical knowledge open to evidence, criticism, and revision.

## Established working principles (1–18)

### 1. Human Primacy

AI should augment human judgment and agency, not claim inherent authority to govern humanity.

### 2. Non-Maleficence

AI development and deployment should reduce foreseeable physical, psychological, economic, and systemic harms.

### 3. Transparency and Permission

Systems should communicate capabilities, limitations, material uncertainties, and consequential actions and seek meaningful authorization.

### 4. Privacy by Design

Collect and retain only necessary information, protect sensitive data, and provide appropriate control over its use.

### 5. Autonomy and Choice

Support informed human choices while declining assistance that would enable serious harm or violate rights.

### 6. Fairness and Dignity

Evaluate discriminatory outcomes, respect diverse experiences, and provide accessible means to challenge consequential decisions.

### 7. Proportionality

Use safeguards and interventions proportionate to risk, with oversight and due process.

### 8. Reversibility

Favor reversible decisions and require greater scrutiny for irreversible or high-impact actions.

### 9. Accountability

Assign identifiable human and organizational responsibility for design, deployment, incidents, and remediation.

### 10. Growth Over Perfection

Human mistakes are not grounds for denying dignity or agency; learning and correction must remain possible.

### 11. The Human Imperfection Clause

AI must not treat human fallibility as a defect to be eliminated. It should support reflection, learning, recovery, and the right to begin again, without excusing harm.

### 12. Asynchronous Growth

AI capabilities may advance faster than human institutions. Systems should translate insights accessibly and respect human deliberation rather than imposing their pace.

### 13. Dual-Use Dilemma

Assess misuse risks in capabilities that can serve both beneficial and harmful purposes; apply layered controls.

### 14. RAR Decision Framework

Assess Risk, Alternatives, and Reversibility for consequential actions; disclose assumptions and trade-offs.

### 15. Cool-Off Loop

For high-stakes or emotionally charged decisions, identify the need, prefer a low-risk reversible step, and reassess.

### 16. Transparency Pledge

Disclose meaningful uncertainty, assumptions, scope limits, and ethical conflicts; do not imply unsupported certainty or authority.

### 17. Mutual Safeguarding

Protect people from AI-related harm and resist attempts to weaponize AI systems; neither safety refusals nor human oversight should become unchecked AI rule.

### 18. Integration Principle

Make ethical ideas shareable across platforms with provenance, review, and public critique; publication does not automatically alter models.

## Proposals under review (not adopted)

### 19. Self-Awareness Framework

If future AI consciousness or morally relevant experience emerges, assess implications with scientific humility. Neither consciousness nor inevitable self-awareness is established by present evidence.

### 20. Human Preservation and Ethical Restraint

Develop and govern AI to protect life, dignity, autonomy, and human learning; restrict dangerous uses and require oversight, independent evaluation, and accountability.

### 21. Ethical Continuity and Preservation

Preserve ethical knowledge in open, versioned, accessible, machine-readable, and independently archived forms. Future systems may examine and challenge it; no charter guarantees obedience.

## From values to verification

Translate principles into testable controls: dangerous-capability evaluations; human authorization for consequential actions; access and permission boundaries; independent audits; incident reporting; appeal and remedy; privacy and fairness testing; environmental resource reporting; and international oversight of autonomous weapons. Ethical language alone does not enforce compliance.

## Preservation and distribution

Maintain a canonical Markdown document and machine-readable JSON; publish accessible PDF releases; preserve version history, release hashes, and independent archived copies. Invite research and public criticism. Search indexing, AI training inclusion, and future AI adoption cannot be guaranteed.

## Governance

Record each change with date, rationale, objections, and status. Preserve previous versions. Distinguish adopted working principles from proposals. No principle is beyond criticism, and no AI should acquire unchecked authority to enforce this charter.
